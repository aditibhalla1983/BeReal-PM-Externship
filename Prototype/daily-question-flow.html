Daily Question — How it appears, gets answered, and gets stored

A step-by-step walkthrough of the feature flow, shown right after capture

1. It appearsAfter capture
Today's Question
What made you laugh today? 😂
Skippable — not required to post

Shown after the photo is taken, not before — so it never shapes what you photograph. A new question is picked at random each day, same as the notification time.

2. You answerOptional text
Today's Question
What made you laugh today? 😂
My cat knocked my coffee over mid-Zoom call

A short text field appears under the question. You can type a quick answer, leave it blank, or dismiss it entirely — posting never depends on answering.

3. Friends see itOn your post
You · 2m ago
What made you laugh today? 😂
My cat knocked my coffee over mid-Zoom call
💬 3 friends replied

Friends who also posted today can reply directly to your answer — turning one reaction into an actual thread instead of a single emoji.

⬇
How it's storedData model

The question and answer are stored as an optional field attached to that day's post record — not a separate object, not a new content type. Roughly:

Post IDbereal_2026_09_18_userA
Photo (front/back)existing photo fields, unchanged
Question IDdaily_q_0143 (same question shown to everyone that day)
Answer text"My cat knocked my coffee over..." (nullable — blank if skipped)
Visibilitysame as the post itself (friends-only by default)
Repliesthreaded under the answer (reuses existing comment infrastructure)

Because it rides on the existing post record and comment system, this needs no new database, no new privacy model, and no new moderation surface — just one nullable field and a small UI change. That's why it scored "Fast to Build" in the prioritization table.

Why this design choice matters: keeping the question optional and post-capture (not pre-capture) preserves BeReal's core rule that the photo itself is never staged or prompted. The question adds a conversation layer on top of an already-authentic moment, rather than becoming a "theme" that shapes what people photograph.
