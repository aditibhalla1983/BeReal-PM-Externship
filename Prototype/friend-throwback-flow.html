Friend Throwback — How it appears, works, and is stored

Resurfaces a past moment involving a friend you interact with often

1. It appearsOccasional, not daily

Shown occasionally · tap to open, swipe to dismiss

Surfaces every week or two — not daily like On This Day — and only for a friend you actually interact with often (reactions, comments, RealGroup chat), so it feels personal rather than random.

2. You interactOpen & respond
You & Maya, 4 months agoPosted the same day, 3 hours apart

Tapping opens both your moment and Maya's from that day side by side. You can react, or jump straight into a chat with Maya — reusing the existing 1:1/RealGroups chat rather than a new messaging surface.

3. It's savedAdded to Memories
Memory Calendar · This Week
You & Maya, 4 months agoSaved to your Memory Calendar

Once surfaced, the paired moment is saved into your Memory Calendar alongside your own daily posts — so it's not a one-time popup, it becomes something you can revisit later, the same way any other post lives in your history.

How it's storedData model

This one needs slightly more than a pure read query — it needs a lightweight interaction-frequency signal to pick a meaningful friend, but still no new content type.

Candidate friendderived, not stored per-post (computed periodically from reaction/comment/chat frequency between you two)
Candidate momentSELECT posts WHERE (user = me OR user = friend) AND date BETWEEN [range]
Pairing logicsame day or same week (reuses the "posted around the same time" logic from Same Moment/Duet)
Surface frequencyevery 1–2 weeks (app-side scheduling, not a new backend job)
Persistence after viewsaved as a Memory Calendar entry (linked pair of post IDs, same calendar that already holds your own daily posts)

The two new pieces of infrastructure are the friend-interaction-frequency score and a paired-entry type in the Memory Calendar (a lightweight link between two existing post IDs, not a new post itself) — everything else (photos, timestamps, chat) reuses existing data. That's more work than Friend Check-In or Reply Prompt, but far less than something like the Friend's Duet pairing feature, which needs a live real-time match instead of a periodic background computation.

Why this design choice matters: keeping the surfacing infrequent (every 1–2 weeks, not daily) and scoped to friends you actually interact with is what keeps this feeling like a genuine, occasional surprise rather than another daily notification competing for attention. Saving it into the Memory Calendar afterward means the moment isn't lost once you swipe past it — it becomes a lasting part of your history, same as any other post.
